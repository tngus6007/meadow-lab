# Educator guide — Meadow Lab

Designed for around age nine, with individual differences and adult/peer support. Prerequisites: comparing small numbers and reading simple arrows. Introduce the optional definitions on the welcome screen: producer, herbivore, predator and support. Intended session length 10–15 minutes is a design estimate, not a measured learning outcome.

## Objectives

Identify two producers (grass and white clover), two herbivores (rabbit and field vole) and a predator (red fox) in a coherent temperate grassland/woodland-edge setting. Trace sunlight and water's role in plant growth and food's role in consumer support. Predict a change, inspect evidence, and explain a repair with limitations. This is practice with a simplified model, not assessment or ecological advice.

## Walkthrough and timing

1. **Healthy example, 1 minute.** Start with all support scores at six and water/light at six. The welcome demonstration explains why every indicator reaches seven after a step. Arrows consistently mean food to consumer; water is a resource and light an energy input, not things foxes eat.
2. **Build, 2–3 minutes.** Remove grass, advance and read the vole journal; add it back. Or remove all species and assemble them again. Learners must include all five with adequate support and identify a valid food path to complete the activity. This teaching completion condition is not a claim that real meadows require exactly these five species.
3. **Predict and experiment, 3 minutes.** Three resettable starts: sunny meadow (6/6), dry spell (water1/light6), deep shade (water6/light1). Predict total plant support rising/falling/staying, or skip. Step and compare the displayed before/after values; a mismatch invites another idea, not punishment. Lower resources affect plants first; later steps propagate food support to consumers. Try several steps and inspect each row, including unchanged saturated/zero indicators.
4. **Restore, 2–3 minutes.** Missing-grass scenario: clover/rabbit remain, but the vole's model food source is absent. Adding grass, maintaining resources and advancing steps restores support by the documented rules. Correct words alone do not complete the task: the habitat must actually reach the stated support threshold.
5. **New patch, 2–4 minutes.** Both light and water are two, plants already weak. Learners must change both resources and advance long enough for the chain to recover. One-step repair is not enough; a consumer can decrease initially while plants recover. The completion summary acknowledges this is not a guaranteed real-world intervention. Paper follow-up asks learners to explain a food path and a missing model factor.

## Adaptation and co-play

Use the healthy example and just one plant/rabbit food path first if reading or working-memory load is high. An adult can read journal rows aloud, or partners alternate making a prediction and stepping. Keep the simulation paused for discussion. Use labels and numerical values, not meter colour alone. Extend by removing both rabbit foods, explaining the delayed fox response, or asking why the model does not represent competition. No personal details, animals or children need to be observed or tested. Stop early or revisit activities freely; progress is not a grade.

## References and what they support

- Royal Horticultural Society, **How Plants Use Light for Growth**, https://www.rhs.org.uk/advice/understanding-plants/how-plants-use-light-to-grow . Supports teaching plants' use of light and the role of photosynthesis, not our invented numerical thresholds.
- Woodland Trust, **Field Vole (Microtus agrestis)**, https://www.woodlandtrust.org.uk/trees-woods-and-wildlife/animals/mammals/field-vole/ . Supports the grass-based feeding link and grassland/woodland-edge context. The model simplifies a wider diet.
- Woodland Trust, **Fox (Vulpes vulpes)**, https://www.woodlandtrust.org.uk/trees-woods-and-wildlife/animals/mammals/fox/ . Supports rabbits/voles as possible prey; foxes have a much wider real diet than the two links shown.
- The Wildlife Trusts, **Rabbit**, https://www.wildlifetrusts.org/wildlife-explorer/mammals/rabbit . Supports the grazing herbivore in the habitat. Grass/clover are illustrative forage choices, not a complete diet.

No source text or media is reproduced. Original simplified rules and pictures are described in README. The model does not conserve matter/energy or simulate birth/death, food depletion or competition. All thresholds, rates and step durations are invented. Light/water levels are abstract, not physical units. Different producer thresholds demonstrate that species need not respond identically; they are not experimental results. Positive feedback to predators does not imply predators lack effects on prey in reality—those feedbacks are omitted here.

## Offline follow-up

Draw grass, rabbit and fox on paper with arrows labelled “food to consumer.” Add water and sunlight beside the grass. Describe what might happen when water is low, then name one factor that could change the real outcome. No field manipulation is suggested. No diagnostic or universal age-suitability claims, and no children participated in testing.
